# Independent business pilot kit

**Status: recruitment and measurement templates only. No independent results collected.**

Keep OrderProof focused on grounded support, human handoff, and verifiable records. Aim for three independent owners with recurring support questions. Three is a practical research target, not a hackathon requirement or a guarantee of any score. OrderProof's founder, fictional demo businesses, and reviewer accounts do not count as independent participants.

## Consent and recruitment

Copy the templates into the gitignored `pilot-data/` directory. Assign anonymous IDs B01–B03; store the identity-to-ID mapping privately. Obtain each owner's agreement before observing work. Explain the pilot purpose, dates, recorded measures, optional feedback, access and retention, and how to withdraw from future participation. Explain that existing sealed chat records follow the product retention policy; do not promise instant deletion of protected archives. Agree separately to publishing the business name, aggregate results, and an exact quotation. Leave unconfirmed consent blank. No outreach is sent automatically.

## Comparable baseline

Before testing, define task families: policy answer, unknown question, human handoff, and retrieving/exporting an earlier promise. Write down task difficulty, information available, timing boundaries, and what counts as success. Compare similar tasks under the existing process and OrderProof. Alternate the order where feasible to reduce practice effects. Do not replay identical answers and present memorization as a product improvement.

Use `observations-template.csv` and the existing report script. Aim initially for ten observations per phase per business; report the actual counts if lower. Keep unanswered questions and failures. Record missing values as missing, never zero. Separate human, actual Bedrock, and fallback answers. While Bedrock is blocked, measure only handoff and record usability.

Supplement observations privately with `task-review-template.csv`: link by business ID, task ID, and phase. Record whether an answer needed correction, the reason, and whether the reviewer checked it against the approved policy. No customer text is required. Do not combine correction counts with citation correctness: they measure different things.

## Outcomes and return usage

Report each business separately before pooled totals. For every measure include its denominator and measurement window:

- Median active handling time, with comparable baseline and product task counts.
- Resolved tasks / all observed tasks; corrected answers / reviewed answers.
- Median handoff delay among completed handoffs, plus pending or failed handoffs separately.
- Successful retrievals and exports / attempted retrievals and exports.
- Optional customer resolution responses and helpfulness ratings, with response count / eligible closed conversations. Nonresponders are not satisfied customers.

Use `followup-template.csv` to record dated return sessions. Distinguish voluntary use, researcher-requested sessions, staff-only testing, and real customer support. Ask once at the agreed follow-up date whether the owner returned independently. Record non-use and its reason. A return login alone does not demonstrate value.

## Commercial interview

Ask the owner, without suggesting a positive answer:

1. Would you continue using OrderProof next week? Why?
2. Which task improved, stayed the same, or became worse?
3. What prevents adoption: answer quality, setup, cost, trust, or another limitation?
4. Show the actual proposed plan and price. Is that acceptable for your business? What would you choose instead?
5. May we publish your exact quotation and the measured results? Show the final wording for approval.

Record the plan, currency, displayed price, interview date, and verbatim response privately. Report willingness to pay as stated interest, not revenue, a sale, or a committed subscription. Stripe remains disconnected.
